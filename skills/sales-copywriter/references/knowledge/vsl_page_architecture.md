# VSL Page Architecture

> **Objective:** Structure the page to support the VSL: focus on the video, proof, CTA at the right timing, and low friction.

## Overview

This document is a focused playbook on **VSL Page Architecture**.

Primary job: structure the page to support the VSL: focus on the video, proof, CTA at the right timing, and low friction.

- Focus: generate pattern interrupts that win attention *fast*, then earn belief with proof.
- Focus: reduce friction, increase trust, and keep message-match from ad → page → decision.

## Key concepts

- Fold 1: headline + video + sound cue
- CTA delay and strategic repetition
- Proof around the video (without competing)
- FAQ and risk reversal
- Trust and compliance elements
- Speed, mobile-first, and video embedders

## Execution framework

1. **Define 1 CTA and the reveal point (delay)**
   - Intent: Define one next step and phrase it as verb + outcome + what happens next; repeat it at the right moment.
   - Output: One CTA phrased as verb + outcome + next step (what happens after the click).
   - Pitfall: Competing CTAs or a CTA that doesn’t match the stage (too much friction too soon).
2. **Write the headline (promise + who it’s for) for the top**
   - Intent: Generate headline variants with specificity (timeframe/number/criteria) and map proof to each claim.
   - Output: 15–30 headline variants with specificity (number/timeframe/criteria) + proof notes.
   - Pitfall: Writing “clever” lines without a clear promise, audience, or proof.
3. **Build a mobile-first layout focused on the video**
   - Intent: Create a mobile-first layout focused on the video in a minimum viable form; ship version 1 fast and iterate based on data.
   - Output: A minimum viable version of a mobile-first layout focused on the video you can ship this week, with clear acceptance criteria.
   - Pitfall: Over-building before validation — you spend time, then discover the bottleneck was upstream.
4. **Insert proof and short bullets below the video**
   - Intent: Collect real proof (screenshots, numbers, demos) and place it with context.
   - Output: A proof pack: 3–10 assets (screenshots, numbers, demos) mapped to key claims.
   - Pitfall: Using proof with no context (no baseline, timeframe, or attribution).
5. **Add FAQ and guarantee before the final CTA**
   - Intent: Define one next step and phrase it as verb + outcome + what happens next; repeat it at the right moment.
   - Output: A guarantee statement with criteria, scope, and expectation alignment.
   - Pitfall: A vague or overly broad guarantee — it increases refunds and attracts the wrong buyers.
6. **Remove distractions (menus, external links)**
   - Intent: Remove distractions that leak attention (menus, external links) so the page has one path to the decision.
   - Output: A distraction-removal checklist (menus, external links, extra CTAs) + a clean single-path layout.
   - Pitfall: Leaving multiple exits (menus, links, extra CTAs) — attention leaks and conversion falls.
7. **Test variations: headline, proof, CTA, and order**
   - Intent: Define the hypothesis, variants, window, and decision criteria before running it.
   - Output: A test brief: hypothesis, variants, audience, window, and decision rule.
   - Pitfall: Changing multiple variables and calling it a test (no learning).

## Templates and examples

```text
VSL page (folds):
1) Headline + video
2) CTA (delay)
3) Bullets + proof
4) Guarantee + FAQ
5) Final CTA
```

## Fold-by-fold reference (VSL page)

### Fold 1 (above the fold)

- Headline: one promise or sharp identification (no generic service title).
- Optional subheadline: qualify who it is for / who it’s not for.
- Video: the hero element (fast load, clean embed).
- Sound cue: a simple “turn your sound on” line.

### Fold 2 (delayed CTA)

- CTA button appears only at the pitch moment (delay button).
- CTA copy: verb + outcome + what happens next (no vague “click here”).

### Fold 3 (proof + reinforcement)

- Bullets: benefits in plain language (1 line each).
- Authority: short bio (contextual proof, not a resume).
- Proof: testimonials/screenshots with context.

### Fold 4 (risk reversal + FAQ + footer)

- Guarantee: clear scope + criteria (avoid blanket promises you can’t honor).
- FAQ: 3–5 objections that stop the purchase.
- Footer: privacy/terms + disclaimers (results vary, no affiliation claims if needed).

## Common mistakes

- Strong claims without proof (or without a realistic mechanism).
- Hiding terms, restrictions, pricing rules, or what happens after the click.
- Using fear/urgency as a substitute for clarity and proof.
- Forgetting required disclaimers/footers (privacy, terms, results vary).

## Tests and measurement

### Metrics

| Metric | What it indicates | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| 3s hold / Thumbstop | Opening strength | Iterate hook category + first frame; keep it 1 line. |
| CTR | Hook + promise match | Test angles/promise specificity; pair with proof. |
| 25%/50% watch rate | Retention | Tighten the first 60s; reduce fluff; strengthen proof cadence. |
| CTA click rate | Pitch clarity | Align CTA to promise; place the button at the pitch moment. |
| Ad disapproval rate | Policy risk | Tighten claims, add disclaimers, and reduce risk language. |
| Negative feedback | Trust + mismatch | Reduce hype; improve expectation alignment; clarify who it’s for. |

### Tests

- Test 5 hook categories on the same angle (keep offer constant).
- Test promise specificity: vague vs specific (number/timeframe/criteria).
- Test proof-first vs promise-first openings (especially on warm traffic).
- Test proof placement: proof before mechanism vs after mechanism.
- Test CTA timing: earlier button reveal vs later (keep script constant).
- Test claim strength: strong vs conservative wording (same creative) while monitoring disapprovals/feedback.
- Test adding explicit disclaimers vs minimal disclaimers (measure trust metrics and CVR).
