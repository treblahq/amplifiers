# CTA Library

> **Objective:** Choose CTAs by funnel stage and friction level (micro → macro).

## Notes

- This document includes an extra section with ready-to-use CTAs by goal and tone.

## Overview

This document is a focused playbook on **CTA Library**.

Primary job: choose CTAs by funnel stage and friction level (micro → macro).

## Key concepts

- CTAs by stage (follow, click, lead, buy, demo, trial)
- Soft vs hard CTAs (when to use)
- How to make the CTA specific (not “click here”)
- How to reduce friction with micro-commitments
- How to test CTAs without confusing results
- How to align CTA to the promise

## Execution framework

1. **Define the stage and goal for the CTA**
   - Intent: Define the funnel stage and single objective (click/lead/purchase) so the CTA, framework, and proof match the moment.
   - Output: A stage-by-stage plan: objective, CTA, KPI, and audience per stage.
   - Pitfall: Using the wrong goal/CTA for the stage (e.g., selling cold) and blaming the creative instead of the mismatch.
2. **Choose friction level (micro/meso/macro)**
   - Intent: Reduce friction and anxiety: remove steps/fields, clarify terms, and make the next action obvious.
   - Output: A friction audit + prioritized fixes (UX, copy, clarity, policies) with expected impact.
   - Pitfall: Removing friction in the wrong place (or adding new friction) — you lower trust while trying to raise CVR.
3. **Write specific CTAs (verb + outcome)**
   - Intent: Define one next step and phrase it as verb + outcome + what happens next; repeat it at the right moment.
   - Output: One CTA phrased as verb + outcome + next step (what happens after the click).
   - Pitfall: Competing CTAs or a CTA that doesn’t match the stage (too much friction too soon).
4. **Add context (what happens next)**
   - Intent: Add context to the CTA (what happens after the click) to reduce anxiety and increase action.
   - Output: A CTA context-line library: verb + outcome + what happens next (examples by stage).
   - Pitfall: A CTA with no context (“click here”) — anxiety stays high and clicks/conversion drop.
5. **Avoid multiple competing primary CTAs**
   - Intent: Keep one primary CTA (and a clear hierarchy) — multiple primary CTAs split attention and reduce conversion.
   - Output: A CTA hierarchy guideline: one primary CTA per step + optional secondary CTAs (clearly de-emphasized).
   - Pitfall: Competing CTAs or a CTA that doesn’t match the stage (too much friction too soon).
6. **Test 2–3 variations per stage**
   - Intent: Define the hypothesis, variants, window, and decision criteria before running it.
   - Output: A test brief: hypothesis, variants, audience, window, and decision rule.
   - Pitfall: Changing multiple variables and calling it a test (no learning).
7. **Measure CTR/CVR and iterate**
   - Intent: Run short cycles: analyze → pick the bottleneck → test → consolidate learnings.
   - Output: A weekly loop: bottleneck → test → learnings → consolidation.
   - Pitfall: Looking at aggregate numbers without segmentation/cohorts — you can’t see the real bottleneck.

## Library (CTAs by friction level)

Choose the CTA by stage. The colder the traffic, the lower the CTA friction should be.

### Micro (low friction)

- Comment “I WANT” and I’ll send you the step-by-step.
- Save this to use later.
- Reply to this email with “YES” and I’ll help you.
- Tell me your situation in 1 sentence.
- Want me to adapt this to your case? Reply here.

### Meso (lead/bridge)

- Download the free checklist here.
- Take the quiz and get your result.
- Watch the full video (link).
- Join the VIP list to get the plan.
- Book a quick demo (15 min).

### Macro (purchase/decision)

- Click to get access now.
- I want to start today.
- Add this to my order.
- Start free trial now.
- Talk to a specialist.

### CTA rule of thumb

- CTA = verb + outcome + next step (what happens after).
- Avoid 2 primary CTAs in the same asset.
- If the CTA is purchase, your proof must be stronger.
