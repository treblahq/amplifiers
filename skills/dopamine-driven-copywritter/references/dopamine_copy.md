# Dopamine Copy (Rhythm, Curiosity, and Tension)

> **Objective:** Write copy that holds attention with short sentences, contrast, and curiosity loops (without turning into hype).

## Overview

This document is a focused playbook on **Dopamine Copy (Rhythm, Curiosity, and Tension)**.

Primary job: write copy that holds attention with short sentences, contrast, and curiosity loops (without turning into hype).

## Key concepts

- Rhythm rules (short, direct, minimal connectors)
- Curiosity gaps and open loops (how to open and close)
- Pattern interrupts (visual and verbal)
- How to “promise without over-explaining”
- Proof and demonstration in small doses
- Common mistakes: rationalizing, long paragraphs, vague promises

## Execution framework

1. **Choose 1 dominant emotion (fear/desire/status/safety)**
   - Intent: Choose one emotional driver and write in the customer’s language; don’t mix emotions in the same asset.
   - Output: One primary driver (fear/desire/status/safety) + a short language bank (10 phrases the customer uses).
   - Pitfall: Mixing multiple emotions in the same asset (confusing tone and desire).
2. **Open a loop (partial promise + mystery)**
   - Intent: Open a curiosity loop (partial promise + missing piece) and plan the payoff line that closes it with mechanism/proof.
   - Output: An opening line that opens a loop + a planned payoff line that closes it with mechanism/proof.
   - Pitfall: Clickbait loops with no payoff (short-term clicks, long-term distrust).
3. **Cut connectors and long justifications**
   - Intent: Cut connectors and justifications to increase rhythm; keep one idea per line and let proof do the convincing.
   - Output: An edited version with connectors removed (short sentences, punchy rhythm, no over-explaining).
   - Pitfall: Cutting connectors but losing meaning — fragmented sentences with no clear promise.
4. **Create contrast (before/after; common/uncommon)**
   - Intent: Create tension with contrast (before → after, myth → truth, common → uncommon) and keep it concrete and provable.
   - Output: 1–3 contrast frames (before/after, myth/truth) you can reuse across hooks and bullets.
   - Pitfall: Abstract contrast with no concrete mechanism or proof.
5. **Insert micro-proofs (1 line, 1 data point, 1 example)**
   - Intent: Collect real proof (screenshots, numbers, demos) and place it with context.
   - Output: A proof pack: 3–10 assets (screenshots, numbers, demos) mapped to key claims.
   - Pitfall: Using proof with no context (no baseline, timeframe, or attribution).
6. **Close the loop with a simple mechanism**
   - Intent: Close the curiosity loop with a payoff: reveal the mechanism or proof that resolves the missing piece (no new loops).
   - Output: A payoff line that resolves the loop + a simple mechanism/proof line (no new loops).
   - Pitfall: Closing with a vague payoff (or opening new loops) instead of delivering proof/mechanism.
7. **End with a clear, single CTA**
   - Intent: Define one next step and phrase it as verb + outcome + what happens next; repeat it at the right moment.
   - Output: One CTA phrased as verb + outcome + next step (what happens after the click).
   - Pitfall: Competing CTAs or a CTA that doesn’t match the stage (too much friction too soon).

## Templates and examples

```text
Dopamine formulas:
- “I’ve never seen [result] happen this fast.”
- “If you do this once a day, [result].”
- “The mistake that’s causing your [pain]… and nobody tells you.”
```

## Common mistakes

- rationalizing
- long paragraphs
- vague promises
- Long sentences with connectors (“because”, “therefore”) that kill rhythm.
- Opening loops you never close (you lose trust).
- Trying to “sound smart” instead of being sharp and specific.
- Replacing proof with adjectives (“insane”, “revolutionary”).

## Tests and measurement

### Metrics

| Metric | What it indicates | How to use |
|---|---|---|
| Avg watch time | Rhythm and payoff | Cut connectors; close loops; tighten pacing. |

### Tests

- Rewrite the same message with 50% fewer connectors; compare watch time and CTR.
- Test open-loop vs closed-loop hooks (curiosity vs clarity).
