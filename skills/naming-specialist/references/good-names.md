# Good Names - Curated References

Reference library of names the owner of this skill considers well-executed. Use as calibration for taste and pattern recognition, not as templates to copy. Before generating names in any category below, read the corresponding section and extract the underlying pattern, then break at least one convention deliberately.

---

## SaaS

**Owner's core references**: Resend, Linear, Render, Railway, Buffer, Notion

**Extended references**: Stripe, Vercel, Figma, Slack, Loom, Plaid, Retool, Cursor, Rippling, Ramp, Mercury, Deel, Arc, Raycast, Warp, Superhuman, Perplexity, Anthropic

### Why these work

One or two syllables. Real or near-real English words. Evocative, not descriptive. Clean `.com`, no prefix or suffix. Fonetically accessible to non-native speakers. Broad enough to extend beyond the original product.

Notion did not start as a platform. Linear did not market itself as "the fast issue tracker" via its name. Resend does not say "email API" - it says the action the user takes. That is the move: the name carries a posture, not a specification.

### Sub-patterns

**Common word, resignified** - takes an existing English word and repurposes it as a category container.
> Stripe, Buffer, Notion, Linear, Arc, Ramp, Mercury, Slack, Plaid, Cursor, Retool

**Short neologism with clean phonetics** - invented or near-invented, engineered to sound like it could be a real word.
> Vercel, Figma, Deel, Loom, Rippling, Raycast, Warp

**Direct action verb** - the product's core action becomes the brand.
> Resend, Render, Railway (motion/rails metaphor), Superhuman

**Latin/Greek-root coined word** - invented with classical roots to signal seriousness or scale.
> Anthropic, Perplexity, Vercel

### Category operating rules

- Prefer one or two syllables. Three is the ceiling.
- The `.com` must be exact or clearable. Alternative TLDs (`.io`, `.ai`, `.dev`) are acceptable only when the product is early-stage dev-tooling and the owner plans to acquire `.com` later.
- Avoid the `-ly`, `-ify`, `-fy`, `-app`, `-hub`, `Neo-`, `Smart-`, `Get-` patterns. They are category defaults and create no differentiation.
- Avoid underscores, hyphens, and numbers in the primary brand.
- The name should work as a verb in casual sentences: "I Notioned it", "let me Figma this", "Resend handles the email".

## Content channels (podcasts, newsletters, shows, publications)

### International references

Acquired, Lenny's Newsletter, Stratechery, Not Boring, The Pragmatic Engineer, Every, Dense Discovery

### Brazilian references

Flow, Inteligência Ltda, PrimoCast, Braincast, Castro Brothers, Café com ADM

### Why these work

Two distinct strategies, both valid.

**Strategy A - Single-word container**: *Flow, Acquired, Every, Stratechery*. Short, abstract enough to hold any topic over years. Works when the creator is confident the channel will outlive its original theme.

**Strategy B - Creator-anchored or descriptive-with-personality**: *Lenny's Newsletter, The Pragmatic Engineer, Inteligência Ltda, Café com ADM*. Long-tail SEO, immediate clarity, personal brand tie. Works when the creator is the product and trust transfers through the name.

### Category operating rules

- Decide early: is the channel a *publication* (Strategy A - container) or a *personal brand* (Strategy B - creator-anchored)? Do not mix.
- If Strategy A, demand the same SaaS-level discipline: short, distinctive, real or near-real word, clean handle across platforms.
- If Strategy B, the creator's name or a signature phrase is doing the work. The rest of the name should be almost boring on purpose ("Newsletter", "Podcast", "Cast", "Show") to let the personal brand lead.
- Avoid numbers, seasons, or format references in the name itself ("Podcast #1 about X"). These age poorly and constrain extension.

## Agencies

### International references

Pentagram, Wolff Olins, Collins, Mother, Anomaly, Wieden+Kennedy, Droga5, Ueno, Instrument, Huge, Work & Co, Area 17, Basic, Manual

### Brazilian references

AlmapBBDO, África, Talent, Suno, Questtonó, Tátil, Greco Design

### Why these work

The agency name operates differently from SaaS or consumer brands. The agency *is* the product - meaning the name must be an empty container that gets filled with meaning by the work itself. This is why so many strong agency names are either extremely abstract (*Mother, Huge, Basic, Manual, Collins*) or built on a cultural or symbolic anchor (*Pentagram, Anomaly, África, Tátil*).

Founder-name agencies (*Wolff Olins, Droga5, Wieden+Kennedy, AlmapBBDO*) trade off abstraction for authorship. They work when the founders are known or when longevity creates brand equity over decades.

### Category operating rules

- Lean abstract or symbolic, not descriptive. An agency named "FastCreative Studio" has pre-defined its ceiling.
- Short is stronger than clever. *Huge, Basic, Manual, Mother* are not coincidences - they are strategic choices to force the work to carry the meaning.
- Founder names only work if the founders intend to be the face long-term and if the names are pronounceable internationally.
- Avoid category words ("Design", "Studio", "Agency", "Creative") unless used deliberately as a foil to an abstract anchor (*Greco Design* works because *Greco* is doing the heavy lifting).

## Infoproducts and courses

### References (scarce by design)

*Atomic Habits* (book → brand), *The Daily Stoic*, *Ship 30 for 30*, *Write of Passage*, *Default Global*

### Why this section is short

The infoproduct market - especially in Brazil - is dominated by long, descriptive, method-name conventions ("Fórmula de X", "Método Turbo Y", "O Sistema Z Definitivo"). These sell in the short term because they compress the promise into the name, but they age poorly, are hard to defend legally, and limit the creator's ability to extend beyond one offer.

The strong examples above share a different move: they treat the infoproduct as a *brand*, not as a *pitch headline*. *Atomic Habits* is a concept, not a method name. *Write of Passage* is a pun with depth. *Ship 30 for 30* is a challenge framing, not a promise.

### Category operating rules

- Name the *brand* that will hold many products, not the first product's pitch.
- Prefer concept names over method names.
- If the creator is the primary asset, consider creator-name branding (Nathan Barry → Kit, James Clear → Atomic Habits ecosystem) over invented method names.
- A name that would fit on a book cover is usually stronger than one that fits on a sales-page headline.

## Consumer apps

### References

Instagram, Airbnb, Uber, Spotify, Duolingo, Strava, Calm, Headspace, Robinhood, Venmo, Cash App, Discord, Reddit, Pinterest, Snapchat, Threads

### Why these work

Consumer apps need names that survive being shouted in a conversation, typed in a search bar, and said by a 12-year-old to a 60-year-old. The bar for phonetic fluidity is higher than SaaS, because the audience is broader and word-of-mouth is the primary acquisition channel.

Strong patterns:

- **Single-word emotion or action**: *Calm, Headspace, Discord, Threads, Pinterest*
- **Compound with a soft, human feel**: *Instagram, Airbnb, Snapchat, Pinterest*
- **Invented but feels familiar**: *Spotify, Duolingo, Venmo*
- **Cultural or mythic anchor**: *Robinhood, Strava* (from Swedish "sträva" - to strive)

### Category operating rules

- Phonetic universality is non-negotiable. Test pronunciation with non-English speakers.
- Avoid names that require explanation of their spelling (unlike SaaS, where a dev-savvy audience tolerates "Vercel" or "Figma" once, consumer audiences do not).
- The name should survive being mispronounced without losing meaning.
- Think about the verb form early: "I Ubered there", "he Snapchatted me". If the verb is awkward, reconsider.

## Companies in general (non-tech brands)

### References

Nike, Apple, Patagonia, Allbirds, Warby Parker, Glossier, Oatly, Liquid Death, Tesla, Rivian, Aesop, Byredo, Muji, Uniqlo, Kindred

### Why these work

Non-tech consumer brands often pull from symbolic, geographic, or mythic territories more aggressively than tech. The goal is usually to signal a *worldview* before signaling a *category*.

- **Mythic/symbolic**: *Nike* (Greek goddess of victory), *Tesla* (inventor), *Aesop* (storyteller)
- **Geographic or cultural anchor**: *Patagonia, Byredo* (Swedish "by redolence"), *Muji* (Japanese minimalism), *Uniqlo*
- **Provocation**: *Liquid Death* (canned water branded as a metal band - deliberately against category convention)
- **Founder-adjacent**: *Warby Parker* (characters from Kerouac's notebooks)
- **Soft, tactile invented word**: *Glossier, Oatly, Rivian, Kindred, Allbirds*

### Category operating rules

- Non-tech brands have more permission to lean abstract, mythic, or provocative. Use that permission.
- Category breaking is often the point: *Liquid Death* in bottled water, *Oatly* in milk, *Allbirds* in shoes.
- Geographic or cultural anchors work when the brand truly honors that origin. They fail when they are decorative.

## Handle and username discipline

A modern name is incomplete without its handles. The primary brand handle should be identical or cleanly adjacent on every relevant platform: X, Instagram, TikTok, GitHub, LinkedIn, YouTube, Threads.

### Strong patterns

- Handle equals brand name exactly: `@linear`, `@notion`, `@resend`, `@stripe`, `@figma`
- For personal brands: `@firstname` or `@firstnamelastname` with no variation: `@naval`, `@paulg`, `@patio11`, `@levelsio`

### Avoid

- Underscores: `@nome_oficial`, `@real_nome`
- Country suffixes: `@nomebr`, `@nomeBR`, `@nome_brasil`
- Numbers as padding: `@nome01`, `@nome2024`
- "Official", "real", "the" prefixes: `@therealNome`, `@officialNome`
- Emojis inside handles

### Operating rule

Handle availability is a veto criterion. If the primary handle on the dominant platform for the target audience is taken and uncleanable, the name is weaker by default. Treat handle availability with the same weight as domain availability during the validation phase.

## How to use these references when generating names

1. Identify which category the brief belongs to. If it straddles two, read both sections.
2. Extract the underlying pattern - syllable count, word-type (common/coined/compound), semantic territory, handle convention.
3. Generate candidates that live inside the pattern's spirit but break at least one of its surface conventions deliberately.
4. Stress-test candidates against the category's operating rules before scoring.
5. Never present a finalist that fails the category's veto criteria (`.com` cleanability for SaaS, handle availability for consumer and content, phonetic universality for consumer apps).
